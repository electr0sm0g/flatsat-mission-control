A

THE PROJECT YOU ARE STANDING ON — FLATSAT v1.0

FlatSat is a physical, open-hardware “satellite on a desk”, created by PwnSat and Electronic Cats (with the first version made possible by Alex Lynd) to learn space hacking on real hardware in a legal, license-free ISM-band radio environment. It simulates real satellite subsystems — on-board computer, dual RF links, sensors, telemetry — over authentic aerospace protocols (CCSDS Space Packet Protocol), and ships deliberately breakable: an .uf2 firmware that wants to be exploited, and eight documented attacks (00–07) to practise on it. Hardware is open under CERN-OHL v1.2, firmware under GPL-2.0-or-later; PwnSat and Electronic Cats are their authors' registered trademarks.

D

WHAT IS REAL HERE — AND WHAT IS SIMULATED, HONESTLY

REAL

  • The simulator, harness and all 56 PoCs are the byte-identical files from the git repo, executed as real CPython (compiled to WebAssembly — Pyodide, version-pinned) in a Web Worker. Nothing is canned or replayed.
  • A POC OK verdict means the transcript made on your machine, right now hashes to the committed simulator transcript digest — the same one CI re-checks on every push to main.
  • Every PoC was additionally executed against the real flashed FlatSat 1 board; both worlds' transcripts are committed — evidence/ (simulator) and evidence-hw/ (board).

SIMULATED (BY DESIGN)

  • The RP2040 binary itself does not run in your browser. The Python simulator is a bit-faithful twin of the firmware's link and behaviour logic — same frames, APIDs, state machines, fault points (docs/REALISM.md maps them side by side).
  • Orbit, eclipse, battery, attitude and the ground station are mission models, not physics: the bench has no ADCS or EPS — the lab says so out loud.
  • Radio here is a wire tap, not RF: same bytes, no anechoic chamber claims.
